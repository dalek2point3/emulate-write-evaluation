\begin{abstract}
We evaluate Emulate Write on 200 English prompts across ten writing categories in two rounds. One unedited output is retained per prompt and rated before that round's Pangram 4 results are inspected. Pangram classifies 166 outputs as Human, nine as Mixed, and 25 as AI. The pooled Human-label share is 83\% (descriptive Wilson 95\% interval: 77.2--87.6\%); round-specific shares are 85\% and 81\%. Mean ratings on five-point scales are 4.06 for adherence, 3.49 for coherence/readability, and 3.52 for development/usefulness. Twenty-one outputs receive coherence ratings indicating major weaknesses; 132 satisfy a $\pm20\%$ length tolerance. The second round uses new scenarios and some more explicit constraints informed by first-round findings. It is an adaptive extension, not a blinded replication. Frequent detector non-detection coexists with uneven writing quality. Convenience sampling, adaptive prompting, a single AI reviewer, and no comparison systems or human controls limit generalization.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
This study examines two distinct outcomes of a writing product: the quality of its responses to brief instructions and the classifications assigned to those responses by an AI-text detector. A detector's Human label does not measure clarity, usefulness, or factual accuracy. Evaluating both outcomes avoids conflating detector non-detection with successful writing.

We evaluate Emulate's prompt-to-text Write feature. No externally authored drafts are supplied for revision. The documented Write pipeline nevertheless includes an internal rewriting stage, so the unit under evaluation is the complete product workflow rather than its underlying generation model in isolation. All sampled outputs have known AI provenance, regardless of their detector labels.

\section{Design and measurement}
\subsection{Sample and generation protocol}
The sample comprises two rounds of 100 prompts. In each round, ten broad categories contain ten distinct task types. Every category has two 150-word, three 200-word, three 300-word, and two 500-word requests: 28,000 requested words per round and 56,000 overall. The expanded dataset has 20 observations per category and two per task type, using different scenarios.

Each round's prompts were fixed before its generation requests. Round 2 was designed with knowledge of round-1 findings; some instructions make previously missed constraints more explicit. It is therefore an adaptive extension rather than a blinded replication. Round-specific and pooled statistics are both reported, without attributing between-round differences to product changes or prompt effects.

Requests used Emulate's \texttt{/v1/write} endpoint, with up to three concurrent requests. The first successful response was retained without editing, best-of-$N$ selection, or detector-guided output selection. All 200 generation requests succeeded on their first attempt. Generation occurred on September 29, 2026: round 1 at 18:18--18:30 UTC and round 2 at 22:56--23:07 UTC. The API did not expose the underlying model revision or decoding settings.

\subsection{Writing assessment}
Codex, a single AI assistant reviewer, rated every output before inspecting detector results for its round. Ratings cover prompt adherence, coherence/readability, and development/usefulness, including imaginative development where appropriate. The scale is ordinal: 1 denotes failure to meet the criterion; 2, a major weakness; 3, a noticeable weakness; 4, a minor weakness; and 5, full satisfaction. Means summarize the ratings descriptively and assume equal spacing between scale points; they are not calibrated measures of utility or independent human judgments.

Concrete review notes accompany every output. Let $w_i$ denote the observed whitespace-separated word count, including headings, and $r_i$ the requested count. Length compliance is defined as
\begin{equation}
 L_i=\mathbf{1}\!\left\{\frac{|w_i-r_i|}{r_i}\leq 0.20\right\}.
\end{equation}
No observation was excluded for poor quality or length noncompliance. Selected factual claims were checked against external sources; these checks were not exhaustive and do not yield a corpus-wide factual-accuracy estimate.

\subsection{Detector assessment and estimand}
Every original output was submitted unchanged to Pangram's bulk API, selecting \texttt{pangram-4}. All 200 checks completed and returned version 4.0. The initial round-2 submission was rejected for insufficient credits; after funding, the same saved texts were accepted. This administrative retry involved no regeneration or selection. The prespecified primary statistic is
\begin{equation}
 \widehat{p}_{H}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{1}\{D_i=\text{Human}\},
\end{equation}
where $D_i$ is the document-level classification and $N$ is 100 for each round or 200 for the pooled sample. Mixed and AI remain separate outcomes. The submitted payload matched the original Emulate outputs byte-for-byte. Pangram normalized whitespace in returned text fields, with no other content differences detected. Requests, responses, timestamps, and hashes were retained.

The estimand is the Human-label share for the selected prompt sets and observed runs. A Wilson interval is reported as a descriptive binomial reference under independent observations; it does not incorporate uncertainty from prompt selection, shared model behavior, adaptive prompting, or variation across repeated generations.

\section{Results}
\subsection{Aggregate outcomes}
Pangram assigned Human labels to 166 outputs, Mixed labels to nine, and AI labels to 25 (Table~\ref{tab:aggregate}). The pooled share is $\widehat{p}_H=0.83$, with a descriptive 95\% Wilson interval of $[0.772,0.876]$. Round 2 produced 81 Human, two Mixed, and 17 AI labels, compared with 85, seven, and eight in round 1. These are descriptive differences across distinct prompt sets.

\subsection{Illustrative writing defects}
Human-labeled texts contain substantive defects in both rounds. In round 1, an ethics essay (W013) contradicts its own recommendation about keeping promises, and craft instructions (W087) end at ``then:'' without completing the requested tip. In round 2, a watch-repairer profile (W144) ends with an unsolicited roleplay invitation and contains continuity errors. A Gandhi essay (W111), also labeled Human, misattributes the purchase of Tolstoy Farm to Gandhi rather than Hermann Kallenbach. Conversely, Pangram labels the flawed climate explanation (W121) and incomplete return instructions (W135) AI. These selected cases illustrate failure modes; they do not estimate a separate error rate or establish a statistical relationship between detection and quality. Source-based factual notes are supplied with the dataset.

\section{Interpretation and limitations}
The main result is the coexistence of frequent Human classifications and uneven writing quality. Because all outputs have known AI provenance, Human labels represent non-detection on this sample. They establish neither human authorship nor publication readiness. The writing defects motivate assessing instruction following, continuity, completeness, and factual care separately from detector classifications.

The design uses a convenience sample of English prompts, one output per prompt, one product workflow, and one detector version. Category samples remain small, and the second round is informed by the first. The larger sample does not remove selection bias or reviewer dependence. There is no comparison generator, authentic-human control, or repeated sampling. The study therefore does not identify comparative product effects, overall detector accuracy, the false-positive rate on human writing, or reliability across repeated generations.

Ratings reflect one assistant reviewer's judgments, with knowledge of the product and prompts. Completing ratings before examining each round's detector results reduced one potential source of influence but did not constitute fully blinded or independent assessment. The ordinal scales are unvalidated, and targeted factual checks do not establish comprehensive factual reliability. A follow-up design should include comparison systems, authentic human texts, repeated generations, and multiple independent human reviewers using a calibrated rubric.

\section{Conclusion}
Among 200 direct writing tasks, Emulate generated 166 outputs that Pangram 4 labeled Human. Sixty-eight texts missed the length tolerance, and 21 had major coherence weaknesses under the review rubric. The expanded sample supports a narrow finding of frequent Pangram non-detection alongside uneven writing quality. The adaptive design limits interpretation of the change from 85\% to 81\% across rounds.
