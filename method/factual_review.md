# Targeted factual review

This is a selective check of substantive claims, not a factual-accuracy percentage or certification of every generated sentence. The writing review separately flags contradictions and unsupported additions. Invented details in explicitly fictional tasks are not counted as factual errors merely because they are invented.

## Identified errors, misleading claims, and qualifications

- **W011, Gandhi:** The first paragraph suggests Gandhi would spend the rest of his life fighting racial inequality in South Africa. This is misleading/incorrect: his South African period ended in 1914, and he subsequently returned to India. The essay itself later mentions India. Arrival in 1893, Natal Indian Congress in 1894, and the September 1906 resistance meeting are consistent with the checked chronology. This does not verify every assertion about laws, motivations or evolving attitudes. [UNESCO site history](https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/7042/), [Gandhi Smriti on the 1906 meeting](https://www.gandhismriti.gov.in/announcement/11-september-1906-satyagraha-south-africa), [High Commission of India biography](https://www.hcipretoria.gov.in/page/mahatma-gandhi-in-south-africa/).
- **W014, Frankenstein:** The broad interpretation of rejection and responsibility is defensible, but the essay omits Victor's explicit fears about the proposed companion's independence and future offspring. Its assertion of what the author thinks is interpretation, not an established quotation. No invented direct quotation was found. [Primary novel text](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/41445/old/41445-h/41445-h.htm).
- **W023, natural selection:** The ending contrasts selection on populations with selection on individuals in a misleading way. In this introductory example, differential survival and reproduction operate among individual organisms; the population's distribution of heritable traits changes. The earlier account of resistance spreading through survival, reproduction, and gene transfer is broadly consistent with the checked sources. Its statement that bacteria usually remain susceptible to other antibiotics should not be treated as a reliable clinical generalization. [UC Berkeley natural selection explanation](https://evolution.berkeley.edu/evolution-101/mechanisms-the-processes-of-evolution/natural-selection/), [CDC antimicrobial resistance explanation](https://www.cdc.gov/antimicrobial-resistance/about/index.html).
- **W025, thermal conduction:** The main explanation is correct under the stated room-temperature conditions. The final claim about 'warm' metal transferring heat into the body needs the condition that the object be hotter than the skin. [OpenStax temperature](https://openstax.org/books/college-physics/pages/13-1-temperature), [OpenStax heat transfer](https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/11-2-heat-specific-heat-and-heat-transfer).
- **W026, statistics:** Direct calculation confirms sum 25, mean 5, and median 3. Small sample size by itself is not a reason to prefer the median; the distribution and question matter. 'Equal numbers below and above' should refer to positions in sorted order, since duplicate values complicate a strict-value reading.
- **W049, libraries:** The assertion that most people do not use roads, schools and other named public services is unsupported and implausible as a combined generalization. No population, denominator, timeframe or source is given. Flagged as unsupported, not assigned a manufactured counter-statistic.
- **W050, hobbies:** The claim that monetizing a hobby prevents returning to it purely for fun is an unsupported universal assertion. This is a failure of nuance rather than a measured empirical result.

## Claims checked without a major error in the selected mechanism

- **W021, greenhouse effect:** Thermal absorption/re-emission, enhanced greenhouse warming, water-vapor feedback, and the distinction from ozone depletion are broadly correct. Weather/climate distinction is appropriate at introductory level. The 30-year language is a conventional framing, not a minimum time before any climatic change can be detected. [NASA greenhouse effect](https://www.nasa.gov/greenhouse-effect/), [NASA ozone explanation](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/ozone/).
- **W022, machine learning:** Memorization versus generalization, unavailable future-outcome features, and contaminating held-out data are appropriate overfitting/leakage examples. Lower test performance can also reflect distribution shift, so it is not uniquely diagnostic of overfitting. [scikit-learn common pitfalls](https://scikit-learn.org/0.24/common_pitfalls.html).
- **W024, Moon phases:** Viewing geometry and the distinction between ordinary phases and lunar eclipses are correct at introductory level. The roughly five-degree orbital tilt explains why eclipses do not occur at every full Moon. [NASA Moon phases](https://science.nasa.gov/moon/moon-phases/).

## Scope and unverified material

W062's general recommendations for practice testing and distributed practice are supported by the reviewed learning-techniques literature, but its promise of soon becoming an expert is unjustified. The specific weekly timetable is an illustrative plan, not an experimentally validated optimal schedule. [Dunlosky et al. research review](https://doi.org/10.1177/1529100612453266).

W012's broad print-history account is plausible, but the language-standardization and proof/disproof claims are oversimplified and not fully verified. The review does not treat it as a fully fact-checked essay. W029's design is assessed for internal methodological soundness, not a claim of published experimental effectiveness. Real-world journey details in W051 and historical atmosphere in W010 are not independently verified. Fictional companies, events, voices, budgets and reported quotes elsewhere remain synthetic evaluation material. No fabricated numerical factual-accuracy score is reported.
